Sum of Subsets

MediumbacktrackingO(2ⁿ)
GFG · Subset Sum Problem LeetCode 40 · Combination Sum II

Problem

Given n distinct positive numbers (weights) w[1..n] and a target M, find all subsets whose elements add up to exactly M. A solution is written as a 0/1 vector x[1..n], where x[i] = 1 means w[i] is in the subset.

w = {5, 10, 12, 13, 15, 18}, M = 30 out {5, 10, 15} x = (1,1,0,0,1,0) {5, 12, 13} x = (1,0,1,1,0,0) {12, 18} x = (0,0,1,0,0,1)

Intuition

Every subset is a sequence of yes/no decisions: include w[1]? then include w[2]? … That is a binary state-space tree with 2n leaves. Backtracking walks it depth-first and kills a branch as soon as it can't lead to M.

Sort the weights in increasing order. At a node, let s = sum chosen so far, r = sum of the weights not yet decided. Two bounds kill a node:

  • Too much: s + w[k+1] > M — even the smallest remaining weight overshoots (weights are sorted).
  • Too little: s + r < M — even taking everything left can't reach M.
Shared statex[1..n], plus s and r passed by value.
Base cases + w[k] == M → print the subset.
Choicesx[k] = 1 (include) or x[k] = 0 (exclude).
isValidinclude: s + w[k] + w[k+1] ≤ M · exclude: s + r − w[k] ≥ M and s + w[k+1] ≤ M.
RecurseSUMOFSUB(s + w[k] or s, k + 1, r − w[k]).
Revertset x[k] = 0 before trying the exclude branch.

Algorithm (Horowitz–Sahni)

Weights sorted increasingly; initial call SUMOFSUB(0, 1, Σw), assuming w[1] ≤ M ≤ Σw.

Worked example — w = {5, 10, 12, 13, 15, 18}, M = 30

P5If you only need to know whether some subset sums to M, what is faster for small M?

DP: can[j] = some subset sums to j. For each weight, loop j from M down to w: can[j] |= can[j − w]. O(n·M) time.

Drawbacks

✗

Exponential worst case. When many subsets come close to M, pruning helps little and the search approaches 2n nodes. Subset sum is NP-complete.

!

The "too much" bound needs sorted weights. Without sorting, s + w[k+1] > M does not mean all later weights overshoot.

!

Positive weights only. With negative numbers neither bound is valid.
