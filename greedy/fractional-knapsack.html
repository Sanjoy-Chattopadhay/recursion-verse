Problem 01 · greedy by ratio

Fractional Knapsack

MediumgreedyO(n log n)
GFG · Fractional Knapsack LeetCode 1710 · Max Units on a Truck

Problem

We have a bag that can carry at most M units of weight, and n items, each with a weight w[i] and a profit p[i]. We may take any fraction of an item (taking a fraction x of item i gives x·p[i] profit and uses x·w[i] weight). Maximise the total profit without exceeding the capacity.

M = 60 w = [5, 10, 15, 22, 25] p = [30, 40, 45, 77, 90] out 230
M = 20 w = [18, 15, 10] p = [25, 24, 15] out 31.5

Intuition

Think of the items as bags of gold dust of different purity. Every unit of space in the bag should hold the most valuable dust per unit. That value is the ratio p[i] / w[i] — profit per kilogram.

So fill the bag with the highest-ratio item first, then the next, and so on. When the next item does not fit fully, take just the fraction that fills the remaining space. Because items can be cut, no space is ever wasted.

i

Why it is optimal (exchange argument): suppose an optimal bag holds some low-ratio weight while a higher-ratio item is not fully taken. Swap one unit of the low-ratio weight for one unit of the high-ratio item: the weight stays the same and profit goes up (or stays equal). Repeat until the bag looks exactly like the greedy one.

Solution technique

Compute the ratio p[i] / w[i] for every item.
Sort items in decreasing order of ratio.
Go through the items: if the item fits completely, take all of it and reduce the capacity.
Otherwise take the fraction remaining / w[i], add remaining × ratio to the profit, and stop — the bag is full.

Practice problems

P1M = 15. Items (p, w): (5, 1), (10, 3), (15, 5), (7, 4), (8, 1), (9, 3), (4, 2). Find the maximum profit.

Ratios: 5, 3.33, 3, 1.75, 8, 3, 2. Order: item 5 (8), item 1 (5), item 2 (3.33), item 3 (3), item 6 (3), item 7 (2), item 4 (1.75).

Take 5 → profit 8, cap 14 · item 1 → 13, cap 13 · item 2 → 23, cap 10 · item 3 → 38, cap 5 · item 6 → 47, cap 2 · item 7 (all 2) → 51, cap 0. No fraction needed.

P2M = 50. Items (w, p): (10, 60), (20, 100), (30, 120). Compare the fractional answer with the 0/1 answer.

Fractional: take items 1 and 2 (60 + 100), then 20 of 30 of item 3 (80) → 240. 0/1 (no cutting): best is items 2 + 3 → 220; greedy-by-ratio would give only 160.

P3M = 16. Items (w, p): (6, 6), (10, 2), (3, 1), (5, 8), (1, 3), (3, 5). Find the maximum profit.

Ratios: 1, 0.2, 0.33, 1.6, 3, 1.67. Order: (1, 3), (3, 5), (5, 8), (6, 6), (3, 1), (10, 2). Take 1 → 3, cap 15 · 3 → 8, cap 12 · 5 → 16, cap 7 · 6 → 22, cap 1 · 1 of 3 of item (3, 1) → 22.33. Answer ≈ 22.33.

P4Why do we compare p[a]·w[b] > p[b]·w[a] in the C++ code instead of p[a]/w[a] > p[b]/w[b]?

It avoids floating-point rounding errors and division. Since weights are positive, cross-multiplying keeps the inequality the same. Use long long so the product does not overflow.

Drawbacks

✗

Fails for 0/1 knapsack. If items cannot be cut, the ratio rule can leave space unused and miss the best combination (see P2). 0/1 knapsack needs DP in O(n·M).

!

Real items are often indivisible. Fractional knapsack models things like gold dust, liquids, bandwidth or time — not laptops or boxes.

!

Floating-point answers. The answer may be a fraction; print with enough precision, and avoid comparing ratios with ==.

!

Only one constraint. With two constraints (weight and volume), the ratio rule no longer works.
